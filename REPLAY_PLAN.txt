1. WHAT "REPLAY" IS HERE, AND HOW THE SYSTEM SEES IT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Data: data/raw/syncan_test_replay.csv (SynCAN "playback"). 93 attack intervals, each 420-831 ticks
  (4.2-8.3 s; 1 tick = 0.01 s), at least 21 s apart, about 13% of rows. Ground truth = the Label column
  (it labels every ID's rows during an attack, so it does not say WHICH signal was replayed).
- Branch 1 (TCN forecaster -> one detector score per tick = max over signals and channels signed / stale /
  frozen / partner, one threshold from normal data) decides "something is wrong". Branch 2 (message-rate
  limits) is irrelevant for replay. Fusion is OR. classify.py then names each alert; "replay" is the
  label an alert gets when no other rule fires.
- A replayed signal carries real, believable values from another moment of driving. Its own history looks
  fine, so a forecaster that mostly reads the signal's own history is only surprised at the start of the
  attack. The other IDs still describe the TRUE present state, so the attack shows up as a mismatch
  with them (this is what the "partner" channel does, but only for 12 of 20 signals).
- Not usable: copies of earlier values. docs/10-detector.md found the replayed values are not repeats of
  anything in the normal files, and a quick extra probe (20-frame windows, same file) was inconclusive.

4. DIAGNOSIS (what limits replay today) -- measured on the cache
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
a) Detection is fast. Onset delay of the first flagged tick: median 4 ticks, 90th percentile 11, max 26.
   So the 5,308 missed ticks are NOT a slow start.
b) The misses are concentrated: 17 of 93 attacks hold 4,048 of the 5,308 missed ticks. In those the score
   peaks early (e.g. event 15: 28.7x the threshold) and then FADES back under the threshold while the
   attack goes on for hundreds more ticks (a replayed signal soon follows a plausible path, so forecast
   error vanishes). Two attacks (events 79 and 5, at ticks 375,756 and 31,825) never rise above 0.84x and
   1.49x the threshold: essentially no evidence.
c) The extra (false-positive) ticks: 3,642 of 3,793 lie within 120 ticks of an attack = the 50-tick
   running-mean window keeps the score high after the attack ends (median tail 46 ticks). Only about 150
   ticks are isolated false alarms (5 false-alarm alerts).
d) Channels on flagged replay ticks: signed residual 86%, partner 69%, stale 0%, frozen 0%.
e) In quiet traffic the score already sits at 0.5-0.8x the threshold (the threshold is set just above
   normal's ceiling), so there is NO headroom to recover a fading attack by lowering a line.
f) Naming errors (13 of 137): 9 called plateau (blamed signals id6_sig1, id4_sig1, id2_sig1: signals that
   are flat for 83% / 31% / 64% of normal time, so a replay of a flat stretch looks like a plateau), 4 called
   drift (a smooth ramp was seen). Earlier naming-rule tuning on main netted ~ -5 alerts: the rule set is
   at its ceiling.
g) Partner channel covers 12 of 20 signals (indices 0,2,4,5,6,7,8,10,13,14,18,19; ridge on cross-ID
   partners, R2 >= 0.95 on held-out normal). The other 8 signals rely only on the TCN's own forecast.
h) Attack spacing and length (needed for any grouping idea): attacks last 4.2-8.3 s, >= 21.5 s apart.

5. PHASE 1 -- CHEAP FIXES, NO RETRAINING  (DONE; small gain)

Results (second half in brackets; baseline in the table above)
  Alert start/stop refinement (walk back while score > 0.6*tau for <= 50 ticks, trim last 25 ticks;
  ported from main's runs.refine_alert_bounds):
      whole file tick P/R/F1 0.963/0.899/0.930, strict F1 0.977, naming 123/137 = 89.8%
      second half tick F1 0.925 (was 0.920). Precision up, recall down. Uses hindsight: it improves the
      tick-level scores, not the real-time delay.
  Dead ends (do not retry the same way):
   - Drop short & weak incidents to remove false alarms: no setting keeps every first-half attack; short
     weak alerts include real attacks.
   - Name each incident once from the union of its alerts (gap 0-1500 ticks): no gain; 0 was chosen.
   - Hysteresis (keep an alert open while score > beta*tau): beta 0.7 equal/worse; beta <= 0.5 floods the
     file with false positives (see e) above).
   - "A plateau holds the value from before, a replay jumps" onset clue: FAILS. Real plateau alerts jump
     at their start just like replays (median |change| 0.15 in both); SynCAN's plateau holds a new
     constant value.
   - Detector window / false-alarm budget grid (window 50/75/100/150 x 0.03/0.1/0.3 alerts per min):
     the current window 50, 0.1/min is already the best for replay on the first half (refined tick F1
     0.930). 0.03/min gives 0 false alarms and strict F1 0.989 but loses 2 attacks and tick recall.
Conclusion of Phase 1: post-processing can reach about tick F1 0.93. New evidence is needed.

6. PHASE 2 -- A REPLAY-AIMED TCN  (TO DO; needs the GPU machine)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Idea: "cross-ID reconstruction". Train a TCN that predicts one CAN ID's values from the OTHER IDs only,
with that ID's own values hidden. A replayed ID has believable history but is out of step with the other
IDs for the whole attack (not only at its start), and the error points at the replayed ID.

6.1 Feasibility gate (do this FIRST, about an hour; stop here if it fails)
    Fit the masked model briefly (or even a small MLP / ridge on windows of the other IDs) and measure
    held-out NORMAL accuracy per signal (R2 or error / signal range). Specifically for the 8 signals that
    have no partner model today. If few of them reach R2 >= ~0.9, the extra evidence will be small:
    report that and stop. Then, on the 17 weak attacks of section 4b (list them with the scorecard's
    FN-per-event code, see replay_scorecard or the snippet in section 11) check the reconstruction error
    stays well above its normal level through the WHOLE attack. If not, stop.
6.2 Model
    Same family as src/canids/models/tcn.py (98k parameters, 4 dilated blocks, receptive field 61,
    sequence length 50, joint (value, staleness) vector, train_1..4 first 80% for fit / last 20% for
    validation, windows never cross files, same loss recipe). Changes:
      - input: the usual 50-tick window, but ALL channels of one chosen ID (its values and staleness)
        are zeroed, plus a one-hot "which ID is hidden" input;
      - target: that ID's signal values at the window's LAST tick (current-tick reconstruction; align the
        stored residual to that tick like the existing residual cache); loss only on the hidden ID;
      - training: pick the hidden ID at random per sample (10 IDs). One network serves all IDs.
      - inference: 10 passes (one per hidden ID) -> a per-signal reconstruction error for every tick.
    Put it in a NEW file (e.g. src/canids/models/tcn_xid.py) and a new training script; do not edit tcn.py.
    Save as models/tcn_xid_syncan_all.pt. Train on normal data only.
6.3 Plug into the detector as a fifth evidence channel "xid"
    Like "partner": rolling mean over the detector window of |reconstruction error|, per signal, divided
    by a per-signal scale from NORMAL data (same percentile policy), kept only for signals whose held-out
    normal error is small (the analogue of the R2 >= 0.95 gate). Add it through an optional argument so
    the existing four-channel behaviour is unchanged (src/canids/detector.py evidence_channels /
    fit_detector / detect; keep the default off; if you must edit detector.py, say so in the commit).
    Recalibrate the single threshold on the normal files (same 0.1 alerts/min budget, floor 0.01 policy)
    and check normal false-alarm rate on syncan_test_normal.csv (unseen normal).
6.4 Evaluate
    - python scripts/replay_scorecard.py (add the xid cache), first half chosen / second half reported,
      plus injected_replay.
    - Success = tick F1 >= 0.95 AND events 93/93 AND naming >= 95% (section 2), with normal false alarms
      not worse than today.
    - Look specifically at: missed ticks (5,308 -> below ~2,500), the 17 weak attacks, events 79 and 5.
    - Re-run the 4 other attack files only for information; they may get worse and that is accepted.
6.5 Compute estimate
    The teammate's own log (logs/train_tcn_all.log, GTX 1650): ~208 s per epoch over the 4 files
    (9,704 steps of 512), 34 epochs. A same-size model should take about 2 h for the full recipe (the
    log also prints "648.4 min", which looks like wall time including something else; trust the per-epoch
    time). The owner's CPU-only PC: ~0.32 s per step = ~50 min per epoch, i.e. more than a day for the full
    recipe, so do NOT run Phase 2 there (a reduced recipe of ~1,500 steps/epoch x 20 epochs is 3-5 h).
    Inference caching of ~10 files x 10 passes is cheap on a GPU.

7. PHASE 3 -- NAME REPLAY POSITIVELY  (TO DO after Phase 2)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Today "replay" = "no other rule fired". With the "xid" channel, name replay by what it looks like:
the cross-ID error is large and concentrated on one ID, the signal is not frozen and shows no smooth
ramp. Keep suppression / plateau / drift / flooding rules as they are. Try the rule as a new function
in a new file (replay_post.py or a new module), not by editing classify.py, until it clearly wins.
Known hard case: a replay of a naturally flat signal (id6_sig1, id4_sig1, id2_sig1) versus a plateau.
Open question to investigate with the plateau file: where does SynCAN's plateau constant come from
(arbitrary value or a value that occurs in normal driving)? If xid cannot separate the two, the
naming ceiling stays near 95-97% and 99% is not reachable.
Also report "replay" label purity across ALL attack files (needs plateau, drift, suppression, flooding
cached): any alert in those files named replay is wrong by definition.
